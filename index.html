Money that has to answer two questions

Traditional investing asks “what will this earn?” Impact finance also asks “what will this change?”, and it expects an answer to both.

The spectrum of capital

Impact investing

Investments made with the intention to generate measurable social or environmental benefits alongside a financial return, ranging from below-market to market-rate.

What makes an investment “impact”

Many funds call themselves sustainable. Impact investing is narrower, and most definitions rest on three features.

Intention

The investor sets out to cause a specific positive outcome, not just to avoid harm.

Measurement

Outcomes are tracked and reported, so claims about impact can be checked.

Return

Capital is expected back, and often more, which separates it from a grant.

Where the money goes

Impact capital flows through almost every asset class.

Private equity and venture capital
Ownership stakes in companies whose products solve a problem, such as clean energy, affordable healthcare or financial inclusion.
Private debt and microfinance
Loans to small businesses, cooperatives or households that conventional banks underserve.
Green and social bonds
Fixed-income instruments whose proceeds are earmarked for eligible projects, issued by companies, banks and governments.
Real assets
Affordable housing, sustainable forestry, regenerative agriculture and renewable infrastructure.
Blended finance
Structures where public or philanthropic money takes on extra risk so private investors can join deals they would otherwise avoid.

How impact gets measured

A common approach, developed through the Impact Management Project, looks at impact along five dimensions.

  • WhatWhich outcome occurs, and does it matter to the people affected?
  • WhoWho experiences it, and how underserved were they before?
  • How muchHow many people, how deep a change, and for how long?
  • ContributionWould it have happened anyway without this investment?
  • RiskHow likely is it that the impact turns out differently than expected?

The open debates

Impact washing. As the label grows popular, some products use it with little evidence behind it. Independent verification and clear reporting standards are the main safeguards.

Additionality. Buying shares in a listed company rarely changes what that company does. Critics argue real impact comes where capital is scarce, not where it is abundant.

Returns versus impact. Whether investors must accept lower returns for deeper impact is still contested, and the answer depends heavily on the asset class and market.
